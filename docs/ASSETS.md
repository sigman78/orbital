# Material assets

The runtime textures live in `assets/materials/` as PNG files and are tracked in git. Each map is stored at the resolution the renderer uploads (at most 4096 pixels wide); the renderer generates the full mip chain on the CPU at load time. `manifest.json` records, per file, the stored dimensions, colour space, license, attribution, the upstream source URL, its SHA-256 hash and its original dimensions.

`tools/import-assets.ps1` regenerates the whole set: it downloads (or reuses from `.tools/asset-sources/`) the hash-verified upstream files, converts images to PNG with GDI+ high-quality bicubic downscaling where needed, bakes the NASA elevation models into normal+height PNGs through `tools/bake-normal-map.py` (Python 3 with numpy and Pillow), and rewrites `manifest.json`. The committed PNGs are canonical; the scripts document how they were derived and let the set be rebuilt from the same sources.

## Sources and licensing

`stars_bsc5.bin` is the Yale Bright Star Catalogue, 5th revised edition (Hoffleit and Warren 1991; public domain, served by the Harvard-Smithsonian Telescope Data Center at http://tdc-www.harvard.edu/catalogs/bsc5.html), baked by `tools/bake-stars.py` into 32-byte records: 9,096 of its 9,110 entries (the rest lack a position or magnitude) as a direction in the renderer's sky frame (x toward the vernal equinox, y toward the celestial north pole), the linear flux relative to magnitude 0, the linear colour at unit luminance from the B-V index through Ballesteros' temperature formula and a blackbody fit, and the V magnitude. 284 KB.

`milky_way_splats.bin` is the Milky Way as a cloud of 4,096 Gaussian splats on the sphere, fitted by `tools/bake-splats.py` to ESA's Gaia DR2 sky in colour (the 1800x900 cartesian rendering, ESA/Gaia/DPAC, CC BY-SA 3.0 IGO, a share-alike licence: keep the attribution and the licence with any redistribution; the map itself is not stored). The fit grows the cloud in stages from 256 splats, each new centre sampled from the block-averaged residual so it lands on coherent structure, the newcomers narrow and of zero amplitude, all splats refined together after each growth (the recipe developed on the RTX 4080 machine; see docs/galaxy_splatting.md). The bake also buckets the splats by the cells of a 7.5 degree longitude/latitude grid they reach into (a table after the records), and the renderer sums, per pixel of a fractional-resolution pass (`shaders/galaxy.slang`), only its cell's list; the background reads that bilinearly. Nothing is sampled from a map: no texel lattice at any zoom, no star residue, under half a megabyte. The shader then redistributes the dark splats' share into dust filaments by a three-octave fBm whose parameters are Sky panel tunables. Earlier routes, ESO's panorama as spline coefficients and as a splat target, and an analytic galaxy model, are retired.

Solar System Scope's Earth day, cloud and night maps are 8192x4096 upstream and stored at 4096x2048. The gas giant is Cassini's December 2000 cylindrical map of Jupiter (NASA/JPL/Space Science Institute, Photojournal PIA07782, public domain; credit "NASA/JPL/Space Science Institute"), 10 px per degree with full latitude coverage, stored at 3600x1800 after cropping the repeated wrap column and pole row; its colour is reconstructed from the 750 and 451 nm filters. It replaced Solar System Scope's Jupiter, which turned out to be a softened, recoloured copy of the same map at 2048 wide. `gas_flow.png` is not downloaded but baked from it by `tools/bake-flow-map.py`: a wind map (east and south flow in texture units per second, plus a turbulence weight) from the measured zonal jet profile (Porco et al. 2003, Limaye 1986), hand-placed vortices for this albedo and shear-weighted curl noise, along which the surface shader advects the cloud deck. `gas_detail.png` (4096x2048, grayscale) is baked by the same tool: three octaves of noise integrated along the wind's streamlines, so the fine structure the shader adds at magnification is filaments that run with the flow. `gas_relief.png` (2048x1024, east and south slopes with the height in alpha) is the cloud deck's relief (the shader reads the slopes; the height is kept for the record), baked by the same tool since no height map of Jupiter's clouds exists to source: brightness at the band scale as height (zones are high ammonia cloud, belts clearings into deeper cloud, with the tops varying over some 30 km after Galileo's PIA00489), storms and swirls at a smaller amplitude, the vortex list as flat-topped domes (the Great Red Spot at its measured 8 km, the ovals assumed at 4 km), and the filament detail as the finest ridges; the slopes are physical (the shader exaggerates them) and stored square-root encoded rather than as a normal, since the slopes that matter are a few thousandths and an 8-bit normal's quantisation draws stripes at grazing light; the manifest records the height range and the encoding's full scale. `gas_polar.png` (1024x2048, sRGB) and `gas_polar_flow.png` (512x1024) are the polar caps, baked by the same tool: the Cassini map is oblique and smeared above about 75 degrees, so the shader blends toward the pole to an atlas of two azimuthal projections (north above south) of the cap within 20 degrees of each pole, scaled on the sphere by the panel's cap size (1.5 by default). The albedo is the map itself beyond 15 degrees from the pole; nearer, where the map is smeared into spokes, it is a planar projection of a clean patch of the map's own 67 to 77 degree cloud texture at physical scale times 1.5 (a triplanar-style projection for one pole), crossfaded in between 11 and 15 degrees, with a hint of the blue-grey JunoCam shows toward the pole; over that: Juno's cyclone clusters after Adriani et al. 2018 (Nature 555; a central cyclone ringed near 84 degrees by eight of 4,000 to 4,600 km in the north and five of 5,600 to 7,000 km in the south, winds of 55 to 95 m/s), filaments from line integral convolution along their flow, a darker eye and brighter ring per cyclone, a few white pop-up ovals, and a slow folded flow between them. The flow atlas holds the cyclones' winds in the cap's own coordinates so the same two-phase advection turns them. The Earth normal/specular maps are 2K and stored losslessly, and the Mars colour map is 8K stored at 4K. The Solar System Scope maps are licensed CC BY 4.0 and are derived from spacecraft imagery and NASA Blue Marble/geodata. Credit: **Solar System Scope — https://www.solarsystemscope.com/textures/**. NASA/JPL images may be used for any purpose with credit (https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/jpl-image-use-policy/). Poly Haven's 2K **Worn Rock Natural 01** (photography by Dimitrios Savva, processing by Rob Tuytel), **Rock Face 03** and **Rock Boulder Dry** (Rob Tuytel) sets are CC0; each contributes an albedo (desaturated 40 percent at import, since the scans are warm tan and asteroids are greyer), a normal map packed with its displacement in alpha by `tools/pack-normal-height.py`, and a roughness map. Keep the attribution with any redistribution.

The Moon colour map and both elevation models are NASA public-domain data. `moon_albedo` is the 4K LROC colour mosaic and `moon_normal` is baked from the 16 pixel-per-degree LOLA elevation model, both from the **NASA/GSFC Scientific Visualization Studio CGI Moon Kit (https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/4720)**. `mars_normal` is baked from the Mars Global Surveyor **MOLA MEGDR** 16 pixel-per-degree grid served by the PDS Geosciences Node. The baked maps hold a tangent-space normal (red east, green south, matching the Earth map's convention) with the height stored in alpha, normalised over the range recorded in the manifest; slopes are exaggerated 1.5x because at 4K a texel spans several kilometres. Phobos and Deimos are rendered as seeded rocks with the scanned rock materials; no global mosaics of usable resolution are freely available for them.

## Decoding conventions

Decode albedo, gas, moon and night-emission maps as sRGB. Decode normal, specular, cloud-mask and roughness maps as linear data. The Poly Haven normals are OpenGL-style +Y upstream and are flipped to green-down (DirectX) when packed, so every normal map in the set shares one convention. The Solar System Scope Earth normal is DirectX-style (green points south, verified from the map's own mixed partials), which matches the renderer's east/south tangent basis without a sign flip.

Loading is done by `src/assets/image.cpp` (vendored Wuffs PNG decoder; stb_image_write for screenshots) and `src/assets/materials.cpp`, which drives the pixel kernels in `src/assets/kernels.cpp`: sRGB-to-linear conversion, luminance-to-alpha masks, normal-map renormalisation and 2x2 box mip generation. The box filter has SSE2 and AVX2 paths selected at runtime; every kernel is bit-exact with the scalar reference kept in the same file and checked by the kernels test.
