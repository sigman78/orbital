## Proposed engineering choices

The prior plan proposed a conventional Vulkan compatibility path within a NoGraphicsAPI fork, artistic scene scale, a staged runnable implementation, and quality scaling. Treat these as the working direction, subject to the foundation spike. Do not assume compatibility has been established or that newer hardware automatically supports upstream extensions.

HDR means floating-point scene rendering and tone-mapped SDR output for the first release. Native HDR display presentation is deferred. Performance figures in the PRD are targets, not measured guarantees.

## 2026-09-11 — implementation resolutions

- The conventional NoGraphicsAPI fallback is implemented and demonstrated on GTX 1080 Ti; details and upstream pin are in FOUNDATION.md. Demo shaders use this ABI even on newer hardware. This is not a replacement graphics library.
- Default shaders use Slang, offline SPIR-V compilation and scene_shared.h shared data layouts. Compiler binaries are pinned by bootstrap.ps1.
- The material pipeline loads realistic sourced maps with provenance; the earlier procedural texture generator was removed. Textures are stored as PNG and decoded with the vendored Wuffs decoder, so loading has no platform dependency. See ASSETS.md.
- Rendering is linear floating-point HDR internally, with ACES-fit tone mapping and sRGB presentation to an SDR swapchain. Native HDR-monitor output remains deferred.
- Longitude-wrap derivatives are corrected for spherical texture sampling. Temporal reprojection and area-weighted tiny-asteroid billboards address aliasing; residual phase/motion artifacts require the evidence in STABILITY.md, not a blanket resolved claim.
- Scene distances are compressed for an authored fictional-system tour. Giant/Earth radii are substantially differentiated; this is not a true-scale Solar System simulation.
- RTX 4080 quality/performance sign-off remains open. Only GTX 1080 Ti has been exercised locally.
- Per subsequent user direction, belt budgets increased 10x to 35k/65k. Seeded plane-cut polyhedra replace smooth displaced rocks, with split face normals and scanned dielectric PBR roughness. A separate polar cluster index conservatively rejects whole offscreen/planet-occluded regions while preserving original IDs and nested tiers. Existing four mesh-instance batches plus one distant-billboard batch avoid per-object draw calls. This is CPU clustered culling, not GPU indirect compaction or hierarchical-Z occlusion.
- Render prettify pass: the atmosphere march is clipped by the opaque depth buffer so rocks in front of a shell are never blended over. Rock shadows on the giant are replaced by an analytic belt shadow (ring profile shared between geometry::belt_ring_density and the shader). Surface detail is procedural where the source maps run out (scaled by texel magnification): Earth land grain and cloud structure, cloud shadows on the ground, Jupiter zonal drift with domain-warped swirls and layered turbulence, Moon relief and self-shadowing derived from the albedo (broad brightness as terrain, small bright features sunk as craters; no height map is sourced). The belt is thinned into rings, its radial bands orbit at slightly different rates, and every rock tumbles at its own rate. Lens ghosts are soft oblique ellipses along the sun axis instead of rings. The gas map is stored at 2K to hide JPEG blocking.
- Mars and its moons, sourced relief: the body list is data driven (up to eight; the renderer finds the terrestrial, gas giant and desert anchors by class). Mars is at true size relative to Earth with a thin dusty atmosphere; Phobos and Deimos are enlarged and orbit fast enough to watch, drawn as seeded rocks with the scanned rock PBR maps. The Moon and Mars shade from NASA elevation models baked into normal+height maps (LOLA, MOLA) instead of relief guessed from albedo; short height-field traces give crater-wall self-shadowing. Exposure adaptation is centre weighted and asymmetric in real time (fast to close down, slow to open), within about a stop; the final composite adds faint lateral chromatic aberration and midtone film grain.
- Rock library and material: rocks are icospheres displaced by a seeded shape function (lumps, ridged noise, bowl craters with rims, plane cuts) generated at six subdivision levels from one function, so normals and silhouettes agree across levels; 16 shapes times 6 levels are drawn as instanced groups by projected size. Meshes are indexed with shared vertices and shape-function normals, ready for meshoptimizer meshlets and mesh shaders later; tessellation was rejected because the GPU layer has no such stages and parallax covers the close-range relief. Three 2K CC0 rock sets are triplanar mapped in object space with a seeded primary/secondary blend and parallax occlusion on the dominant plane of close rocks.
- GPU-driven belt culling replaces the CPU cluster pass: a compute shader runs one thread per rock (frustum with jitter padding, analytic occlusion by every body, projected-size level pick, billboard demotion), then a count, prefix and scatter sequence writes per-group instance lists and indirect draw arguments that the CPU submits as one indirect draw per group. Everything goes through buffer addresses because the conventional descriptor set has no storage buffers; the fork gained conventional-backend paths for compute pipelines and indirect draws. Depth-pyramid occlusion was not added: the only occluders that matter here are the bodies, which the analytic test covers, and the conventional path has no storage images to build a pyramid with. Measured at the frozen belt view, 1080p, GTX 1080 Ti (`--rocks` overrides the belt size for such runs): at 65k rocks the CPU pass took 1.25 ms per frame and the GPU pass 0.21 ms of CPU; at 650k rocks the CPU pass took 13.5 ms and the frame 18.9 ms, the GPU pass 0.23 ms of CPU and a 6.9 ms frame. The benchmark CSV records the CPU preparation time and the GPU pass timings per frame separately from the vsync-bound frame time; the culling dispatches plus the shadow pass measure 0.09 ms at 65k rocks and 0.25 ms at 520k, so the belt's GPU cost is its rendering, not its culling.
- Belt budgets rose 8x to 280k baseline and 520k high quality once culling moved to the GPU. The rock library is uploaded as one pooled vertex and index range and drawn with a single indexed multi-draw whose per-group slices (first index, vertex offset, first instance) the prefix pass writes; the prefix pass also compacts empty groups out and writes the draw count for `draw_indexed_indirect_count`. Measured at the frozen belt view, 1080p: pooling took the GPU median from 5.13 to 5.06 ms at 65k and from 5.86 to 5.79 ms at 520k and removed the 520k frame-time spikes (p95 10.9 to 7.1 ms) that recording 97 draws caused; count compaction changed nothing measurable, since the GPU already skips zero-instance draws, and is kept because it is free and bounds the multi-draw to live groups. The title bar shows frame time, a rolling p95, GPU time, draw calls, live rock groups, visible rocks and triangles.
- Belt self-shadowing is analytic extinction, not a shadow map. A belt-wide orthographic depth map (4096 texels over the annulus in light space, casters of radius 0.05 and above culled by a second compute pass, 0.41 ms at 520k rocks) was built and rejected: the belt is 1.4 units thick and mostly empty, so rock-on-rock occlusion was rare and only visible on close rocks, and the sun's one-degree disc makes real shadows at these distances mostly penumbra, so the hard 0.03-unit texels shimmered as the casters tumbled. Instead each rock pixel integrates the belt's density (ring profile times a vertical falloff) along its sun ray and attenuates by exp(-tau); rocks on the sunward rim stay bright, buried and far-side rocks sit in a smooth dusk, billboards included. It is stable by construction, consistent with the belt's shadow on the giant, and costs about 0.17 ms in the surface pass. The belt's shadow on the gas giant was strengthened 30 percent at the same time. Screen-space contact shadows were tried and reverted: crisp and stable on rocks directly behind a neighbour, but few rocks qualify in a belt this sparse, for about 0.4 ms. A belt transmittance map sits on top instead (F3 toggles it, F4 the extinction, so the two can be compared live): the size-tail rocks (radius 0.03 and above, about 5% of the belt, kept as a compact second record array so only they run through the splat) add their cross-section additively into a 2048-texel light-space map over the whole belt, into one of four depth slices measured along the sun ray from the texel column's crossing of the belt plane, and a separable blur widens it into the sun's penumbra. A receiver sums the slices on its sun side (never its own, which holds its own splat) and attenuates by exp(-coverage). It is stable by construction and shades rocks behind neighbours softly, but it too is sparse in effect, and it costs about 0.6 ms at 520k rocks (0.4 ms of splat, clear and blur, 0.4 ms of lookup, partly overlapping). Splatting every rock cost 1.4 ms: the vertex work over the whole belt dominated, a 1024 map saved only 0.2 ms and a radius test inside the vertex shader nothing, hence the compact caster list. Small rocks cover under a fifth of a texel each and made no visible difference.
- Belt population shaped after the main belt: the radial profile keeps its three rings and gap but gains a sharp resonance-style inner edge (4% of the width) and an exponential outer tail (e-folding 14% of the width, reaching 1.5 widths) so the belt fades to nothing; heights are Gaussian with a scale height that flares from 0.6 to 1.4 half thicknesses across the width, as inclinations grow outward; sizes follow a power law between 0.5 and 12 reference scales with differential exponent 3 (Dohnanyi's 3.5 made the median rock a sub-pixel speck and the belt vanished at distance); composition follows the taxonomic gradient, bright reddish silicate rocks dominating inside and dark neutral carbonaceous rocks outside, with 3% grey metallic ones, the class chosen per rock by radius and chance, carried in the instance tint's integer part, and also picking which scanned rock set the material blends. The old fixed size tail (4x and 9x by id) is gone; the belt shadow, extinction and transmittance box all follow the same tapered profile.
- Anti-aliasing rework against shimmer on small and peripheral rocks, measured as the pixel difference between two consecutive jittered frames of a frozen scene at 1080p. Three causes: the temporal pass rejected history whenever stored and reprojected depth disagreed, so a pixel flipping between a thin rock and the sky never converged; history blended in linear HDR, so bright specks dominated and flickered; and meshes under two pixels alias by construction. The temporal pass now only rejects on a depth mismatch when both frames saw a surface, clips history to the 3x3 neighbourhood in YCoCg by variance (1.5 sigma), blends with luminance weights, and fetches history with a Catmull-Rom filter; rocks under 2.5 pixels of radius become the analytic disc billboards; and an FXAA pass (edge-end search plus sub-pixel blend) runs over the tone-mapped image through an intermediate so captures include it. F5 cycles off, temporal, temporal plus FXAA (the default), and --aa sets it for scripted runs. Pixels changing by more than 48 levels between frames: distant belt view 917 to 28, dense belt view 4952 to 2110; pixels changing by more than 16: 7894 to 3270 and 17351 to 17285 (the residual is sub-pixel texture motion on close rocks). Cost about 0.6 ms at 520k rocks, mostly the larger billboards, of which FXAA is 0.07 ms.
- Sub-pixel rocks are lit once per rock in the culling pass and drawn as pixel-filter footprints, not shaded discs. The disc billboards spread a rock's brightness over a disc the size of the cut-off, so a 0.3-pixel rock read as a dim 5-pixel blob, and every blob pixel ran the full lighting, extinction and transmittance lookup. Now the cull pass computes the radiance of each splat once: a Lambertian sphere's disc-averaged brightness at its sun-rock-camera phase angle (the analytic phase function, 2/3 at full phase, no bake), times the body eclipse, belt extinction and transmittance map, times the class colour and the average scanned albedo; the fragment only shapes a Gaussian footprint no narrower than 0.8 pixels whose alpha integrates to the rock's true covered area. Splats stay small and dim at distance, the belt resolves as fine grain, and the surface pass drops from 3.50 to 3.01 ms at 520k rocks for 0.13 ms more in the cull pass. Spherical harmonics per shape would add the rock's own view dependence on top and remain an option; the shared belt lighting moved into shaders/belt.slang, which both the surface shaders and the cull pass include. Calibrated afterwards against mesh-only renders (the --splat option scripts the cut-off): the first point splats saturated a full pixel for rocks near one pixel wide, which a mesh only covers some frames, so the far field read brighter as more rocks became splats even though the energy matched within a few percent. Splats now have two regimes, a soft-edged disc of the rock's true size above half a pixel and a one-pixel Gaussian point below it whose peak alpha stays under one; the disc is scaled to the shapes' projected area (0.8 of the bounding disc), the phase function gains a small forward-scatter rim (0.12) standing in for the meshes' grazing specular, and each splat uses the coarsest mip of the scanned set its class leads with. Luminance over the pixels that change with the cut-off, relative to meshes only: far view 0.97 / 0.99 / 0.99 and belt view 0.98 / 1.02 / 1.07 at the 1.2, 2.5 and 4 pixel cut-offs. Disc splats are then shaded as sphere impostors: the cull pass stores albedo times irradiance plus the per-rock rim and ambient fractions, and the fragment takes the Lambert cosine per pixel from the sphere normal the disc coordinate implies, so a few-pixel rock shows a lit side and a dark limb like its mesh. The disc average of that cosine is the phase function the points use, so the calibration holds (0.99 and 0.98 at the 2.5 and 4 pixel cut-offs in the belt view) at no measurable cost. The count pass no longer lights splats at all, since it only needs each rock's group; the scatter pass does it once. F7 switches the lighting back on in both passes for comparison: 0.82 versus 0.75 ms of cull time at 520k rocks, identical images.
- Three tone curves selectable at runtime (F8, --tone): ACES filmic as Stephen Hill's fit of the RRT and sRGB ODT (replacing the Narkowicz approximation), AgX in its minimal form (inset matrix, log2 encode over the working range, sixth-order sigmoid, outset, decode) and Khronos PBR Neutral (hue preserving, linear to 0.8 then a shoulder with slight desaturation). All matrix products are written as row dot products so the column-major layout flag for buffer matrices cannot change their meaning. ACES stays the default; AgX lifts shadows and flattens highlights, Neutral is the most literal and the most saturated.
