## Proposed engineering choices

The prior plan proposed a conventional Vulkan compatibility path within a NoGraphicsAPI fork, artistic scene scale, a staged runnable implementation, and quality scaling. Treat these as the working direction, subject to the foundation spike. Do not assume compatibility has been established or that newer hardware automatically supports upstream extensions.

HDR means floating-point scene rendering and tone-mapped SDR output for the first release. Native HDR display presentation is deferred. Performance figures in the PRD are targets, not measured guarantees.

## 2026-09-11 — implementation resolutions

- The conventional NoGraphicsAPI fallback is implemented and demonstrated on GTX 1080 Ti; details and upstream pin are in FOUNDATION.md. Demo shaders use this ABI even on newer hardware. This is not a replacement graphics library.
- Default shaders use Slang, offline SPIR-V compilation and scene_shared.h shared data layouts. Compiler binaries are pinned by bootstrap.ps1.
- The material pipeline loads realistic sourced maps with provenance; the earlier procedural texture generator was removed. Textures are stored as PNG and decoded with the vendored Wuffs decoder, so loading has no platform dependency. See ASSETS.md.
- Rendering is linear floating-point HDR internally, with ACES-fit tone mapping and sRGB presentation to an SDR swapchain. Native HDR-monitor output remains deferred.
- Longitude-wrap derivatives are corrected for spherical texture sampling. Temporal reprojection and area-weighted tiny-asteroid billboards address aliasing; residual phase/motion artifacts require the evidence in STABILITY.md, not a blanket resolved claim.
- Scene distances are compressed for an authored fictional-system tour. Giant/Earth radii are substantially differentiated; this is not a true-scale Solar System simulation.
- RTX 4080 quality/performance sign-off remains open. Only GTX 1080 Ti has been exercised locally.
- Per subsequent user direction, belt budgets increased 10x to 35k/65k. Seeded plane-cut polyhedra replace smooth displaced rocks, with split face normals and scanned dielectric PBR roughness. A separate polar cluster index conservatively rejects whole offscreen/planet-occluded regions while preserving original IDs and nested tiers. Existing four mesh-instance batches plus one distant-billboard batch avoid per-object draw calls. This is CPU clustered culling, not GPU indirect compaction or hierarchical-Z occlusion.
- Render prettify pass: the atmosphere march is clipped by the opaque depth buffer so rocks in front of a shell are never blended over. Rock shadows on the giant are replaced by an analytic belt shadow (ring profile shared between geometry::belt_ring_density and the shader). Surface detail is procedural where the source maps run out (scaled by texel magnification): Earth land grain and cloud structure, cloud shadows on the ground, Jupiter zonal drift with domain-warped swirls and layered turbulence, Moon relief and self-shadowing derived from the albedo (broad brightness as terrain, small bright features sunk as craters; no height map is sourced). The belt is thinned into rings, its radial bands orbit at slightly different rates, and every rock tumbles at its own rate. Lens ghosts are soft oblique ellipses along the sun axis instead of rings. The gas map is stored at 2K to hide JPEG blocking.
- Mars and its moons, sourced relief: the body list is data driven (up to eight; the renderer finds the terrestrial, gas giant and desert anchors by class). Mars is at true size relative to Earth with a thin dusty atmosphere; Phobos and Deimos are enlarged and orbit fast enough to watch, drawn as seeded rocks with the scanned rock PBR maps. The Moon and Mars shade from NASA elevation models baked into normal+height maps (LOLA, MOLA) instead of relief guessed from albedo; short height-field traces give crater-wall self-shadowing. Exposure adaptation is centre weighted and asymmetric in real time (fast to close down, slow to open), within about a stop; the final composite adds faint lateral chromatic aberration and midtone film grain.
- Rock library and material: rocks are icospheres displaced by a seeded shape function (lumps, ridged noise, bowl craters with rims, plane cuts) generated at six subdivision levels from one function, so normals and silhouettes agree across levels; 16 shapes times 6 levels are drawn as instanced groups by projected size. Meshes are indexed with shared vertices and shape-function normals, ready for meshoptimizer meshlets and mesh shaders later; tessellation was rejected because the GPU layer has no such stages and parallax covers the close-range relief. Three 2K CC0 rock sets are triplanar mapped in object space with a seeded primary/secondary blend and parallax occlusion on the dominant plane of close rocks.
- GPU-driven belt culling replaces the CPU cluster pass: a compute shader runs one thread per rock (frustum with jitter padding, analytic occlusion by every body, projected-size level pick, billboard demotion), then a count, prefix and scatter sequence writes per-group instance lists and indirect draw arguments that the CPU submits as one indirect draw per group. Everything goes through buffer addresses because the conventional descriptor set has no storage buffers; the fork gained conventional-backend paths for compute pipelines and indirect draws. Depth-pyramid occlusion was not added: the only occluders that matter here are the bodies, which the analytic test covers, and the conventional path has no storage images to build a pyramid with. Measured at the frozen belt view, 1080p, GTX 1080 Ti (`--rocks` overrides the belt size for such runs): at 65k rocks the CPU pass took 1.25 ms per frame and the GPU pass 0.21 ms of CPU; at 650k rocks the CPU pass took 13.5 ms and the frame 18.9 ms, the GPU pass 0.23 ms of CPU and a 6.9 ms frame. The benchmark CSV records the CPU preparation time and the GPU pass timings per frame separately from the vsync-bound frame time; the culling dispatches plus the shadow pass measure 0.09 ms at 65k rocks and 0.25 ms at 520k, so the belt's GPU cost is its rendering, not its culling.
- Belt budgets rose 8x to 280k baseline and 520k high quality once culling moved to the GPU. The rock library is uploaded as one pooled vertex and index range and drawn with a single indexed multi-draw whose per-group slices (first index, vertex offset, first instance) the prefix pass writes; the prefix pass also compacts empty groups out and writes the draw count for `draw_indexed_indirect_count`. Measured at the frozen belt view, 1080p: pooling took the GPU median from 5.13 to 5.06 ms at 65k and from 5.86 to 5.79 ms at 520k and removed the 520k frame-time spikes (p95 10.9 to 7.1 ms) that recording 97 draws caused; count compaction changed nothing measurable, since the GPU already skips zero-instance draws, and is kept because it is free and bounds the multi-draw to live groups. The title bar shows frame time, a rolling p95, GPU time, draw calls, live rock groups, visible rocks and triangles.
- Belt self-shadowing is analytic extinction, not a shadow map. A belt-wide orthographic depth map (4096 texels over the annulus in light space, casters of radius 0.05 and above culled by a second compute pass, 0.41 ms at 520k rocks) was built and rejected: the belt is 1.4 units thick and mostly empty, so rock-on-rock occlusion was rare and only visible on close rocks, and the sun's one-degree disc makes real shadows at these distances mostly penumbra, so the hard 0.03-unit texels shimmered as the casters tumbled. Instead each rock pixel integrates the belt's density (ring profile times a vertical falloff) along its sun ray and attenuates by exp(-tau); rocks on the sunward rim stay bright, buried and far-side rocks sit in a smooth dusk, billboards included. It is stable by construction, consistent with the belt's shadow on the giant, and costs about 0.17 ms in the surface pass. The belt's shadow on the gas giant was strengthened 30 percent at the same time. Screen-space contact shadows were tried and reverted: crisp and stable on rocks directly behind a neighbour, but few rocks qualify in a belt this sparse, for about 0.4 ms. A belt transmittance map sits on top instead (F3 toggles it, F4 the extinction, so the two can be compared live): the size-tail rocks (radius 0.03 and above, about 5% of the belt, kept as a compact second record array so only they run through the splat) add their cross-section additively into a 2048-texel light-space map over the whole belt, into one of four depth slices measured along the sun ray from the texel column's crossing of the belt plane, and a separable blur widens it into the sun's penumbra. A receiver sums the slices on its sun side (never its own, which holds its own splat) and attenuates by exp(-coverage). It is stable by construction and shades rocks behind neighbours softly, but it too is sparse in effect, and it costs about 0.6 ms at 520k rocks (0.4 ms of splat, clear and blur, 0.4 ms of lookup, partly overlapping). Splatting every rock cost 1.4 ms: the vertex work over the whole belt dominated, a 1024 map saved only 0.2 ms and a radius test inside the vertex shader nothing, hence the compact caster list. Small rocks cover under a fifth of a texel each and made no visible difference.
- Belt population shaped after the main belt: the radial profile keeps its three rings and gap but gains a sharp resonance-style inner edge (4% of the width) and an exponential outer tail (e-folding 14% of the width, reaching 1.5 widths) so the belt fades to nothing; heights are Gaussian with a scale height that flares from 0.6 to 1.4 half thicknesses across the width, as inclinations grow outward; sizes follow a power law between 0.5 and 12 reference scales with differential exponent 3 (Dohnanyi's 3.5 made the median rock a sub-pixel speck and the belt vanished at distance); composition follows the taxonomic gradient, bright reddish silicate rocks dominating inside and dark neutral carbonaceous rocks outside, with 3% grey metallic ones, the class chosen per rock by radius and chance, carried in the instance tint's integer part, and also picking which scanned rock set the material blends. The old fixed size tail (4x and 9x by id) is gone; the belt shadow, extinction and transmittance box all follow the same tapered profile.
- Anti-aliasing rework against shimmer on small and peripheral rocks, measured as the pixel difference between two consecutive jittered frames of a frozen scene at 1080p. Three causes: the temporal pass rejected history whenever stored and reprojected depth disagreed, so a pixel flipping between a thin rock and the sky never converged; history blended in linear HDR, so bright specks dominated and flickered; and meshes under two pixels alias by construction. The temporal pass now only rejects on a depth mismatch when both frames saw a surface, clips history to the 3x3 neighbourhood in YCoCg by variance (1.5 sigma), blends with luminance weights, and fetches history with a Catmull-Rom filter; rocks under 2.5 pixels of radius become the analytic disc billboards; and an FXAA pass (edge-end search plus sub-pixel blend) runs over the tone-mapped image through an intermediate so captures include it. F5 cycles off, temporal, temporal plus FXAA (the default), and --aa sets it for scripted runs. Pixels changing by more than 48 levels between frames: distant belt view 917 to 28, dense belt view 4952 to 2110; pixels changing by more than 16: 7894 to 3270 and 17351 to 17285 (the residual is sub-pixel texture motion on close rocks). Cost about 0.6 ms at 520k rocks, mostly the larger billboards, of which FXAA is 0.07 ms.
- Sub-pixel rocks are lit once per rock in the culling pass and drawn as pixel-filter footprints, not shaded discs. The disc billboards spread a rock's brightness over a disc the size of the cut-off, so a 0.3-pixel rock read as a dim 5-pixel blob, and every blob pixel ran the full lighting, extinction and transmittance lookup. Now the cull pass computes the radiance of each splat once: a Lambertian sphere's disc-averaged brightness at its sun-rock-camera phase angle (the analytic phase function, 2/3 at full phase, no bake), times the body eclipse, belt extinction and transmittance map, times the class colour and the average scanned albedo; the fragment only shapes a Gaussian footprint no narrower than 0.8 pixels whose alpha integrates to the rock's true covered area. Splats stay small and dim at distance, the belt resolves as fine grain, and the surface pass drops from 3.50 to 3.01 ms at 520k rocks for 0.13 ms more in the cull pass. Spherical harmonics per shape would add the rock's own view dependence on top and remain an option; the shared belt lighting moved into shaders/belt.slang, which both the surface shaders and the cull pass include. Calibrated afterwards against mesh-only renders (the --splat option scripts the cut-off): the first point splats saturated a full pixel for rocks near one pixel wide, which a mesh only covers some frames, so the far field read brighter as more rocks became splats even though the energy matched within a few percent. Splats now have two regimes, a soft-edged disc of the rock's true size above half a pixel and a one-pixel Gaussian point below it whose peak alpha stays under one; the disc is scaled to the shapes' projected area (0.8 of the bounding disc), the phase function gains a small forward-scatter rim (0.12) standing in for the meshes' grazing specular, and each splat uses the coarsest mip of the scanned set its class leads with. Luminance over the pixels that change with the cut-off, relative to meshes only: far view 0.97 / 0.99 / 0.99 and belt view 0.98 / 1.02 / 1.07 at the 1.2, 2.5 and 4 pixel cut-offs. Disc splats are then shaded as sphere impostors: the cull pass stores albedo times irradiance plus the per-rock rim and ambient fractions, and the fragment takes the Lambert cosine per pixel from the sphere normal the disc coordinate implies, so a few-pixel rock shows a lit side and a dark limb like its mesh. The disc average of that cosine is the phase function the points use, so the calibration holds (0.99 and 0.98 at the 2.5 and 4 pixel cut-offs in the belt view) at no measurable cost. The count pass no longer lights splats at all, since it only needs each rock's group; the scatter pass does it once. F7 switches the lighting back on in both passes for comparison: 0.82 versus 0.75 ms of cull time at 520k rocks, identical images.
- Three tone curves selectable at runtime (F8, --tone): ACES filmic as Stephen Hill's fit of the RRT and sRGB ODT (replacing the Narkowicz approximation), AgX in its minimal form (inset matrix, log2 encode over the working range, sixth-order sigmoid, outset, decode) and Khronos PBR Neutral (hue preserving, linear to 0.8 then a shoulder with slight desaturation). All matrix products are written as row dot products so the column-major layout flag for buffer matrices cannot change their meaning. ACES stays the default; AgX lifts shadows and flattens highlights, Neutral is the most literal and the most saturated.
- Per-curve exposure trims, applied to the frame exposure before the curve: AgX 0.37, PBR Neutral 0.75, ACES 1. Fitted against captures rather than a grey ramp, because the curves' toes behave differently on coloured shadows (Neutral's black offset uses the minimum channel, ACES desaturates): the geometric mean of the per-pixel display luminance ratio to ACES over pixels ACES shows at 0.005 to 0.03 was 1.35 for AgX and 1.5 to 1.75 for Neutral untrimmed, and is 1.15 for both (belt view 1.17 and 1.41) with the trims; mids sit at 0.7 (AgX) and 0.88 (Neutral) of ACES, the price of AgX's flat shoulder and of matching the shadows. Untrimmed, the curves compared brightness more than rendering intent.
- Base exposure gain of 1.5 ahead of the tone curves. The Hill ACES fit sits about a stop under the Narkowicz approximation it replaced (scene 0.18 maps to 0.11 display linear against 0.27), so the switch made every view darker; the gain brings the scene back toward the earlier brightness while keeping the user's exposure at 1 and the +/- keys meaningful. The per-curve trims stay, so the curves still match in the shadows.
- Khronos PBR Neutral is the default tone curve (--tone 2), chosen by eye after the base gain: it is the most literal of the three and keeps the belt colours and Jupiter bands saturated. ACES filmic and AgX remain on F8.
- Rock splats are drawn into their own layer (premultiplied rgba16f, slot 31, depth tested against the scene but after the atmosphere) without the jitter, and the post pass composites the layer over the temporally anti-aliased image. Splats faded to nothing while the camera translated: a sub-pixel feature that moves a fraction of a pixel per frame is resampled and clipped out of the history every frame, whatever the reprojection does (giving the splats depth for exact reprojection recovered only part of it, and a lower history weight under motion nothing). Measured on a fixed-rate lateral pan (--pan, a development option that adds a constant to the strafe axis and steps the camera at 60 Hz so runs compare): the dim specks of the no-AA image kept a median 0.30 to 0.35 of their peak and 0.62 to 0.67 of their 5x5 energy under the old pass, 1.00 and 0.99 with the layer; static frames are unchanged. The splats are smooth by construction (Gaussian points, soft-edged discs lit once per rock), so they need no temporal filter; only the depth test against the jittered scene remains, which costs nothing measurable in frame-to-frame flicker (2976 against 2990 pixels changing by more than 48 levels in the dense belt view, mean change 0.87 against 0.93). GPU frame 6.56 ms against 6.77 before at 520k rocks.
- FXAA no longer smears distant rocks: the sub-pixel blend strength drops from 0.75 to 0.5, a dim pixel brighter than all four neighbours (a far rock, not an edge; highlights on close rocks stay above the 0.35 luma cap and keep the filter) passes through, and so does any edge whose end search stops within 2.5 texels, which is a few-pixel rock. Specks in the far belt keep 0.96 of their no-AA luminance against 0.85 before, the same as the temporal pass alone. The price is that the residual flicker of 3 to 4 pixel mesh rocks is no longer blurred away: pixels changing by more than 48 levels between frames rise from 2976 to 4190 in the dense belt view, while the count above 16 levels and the mean change stay level. Raising the splat cut-off (F6) turns those rocks into stable splats. SMAA would be the next step if the trade still shows.
- The splat layer is gone again: taking the splats out of the temporal pass made every sub-pixel rock twinkle as it crossed pixel centres (a one-pixel Gaussian's brightest pixel changes 4x with sub-pixel position, and widening it to 0.6 px sigma still leaves 2x), which reads as embers. The splats are back in the HDR image, jittered and temporally filtered like everything else, and the fade is fixed inside the pass instead: after the atmosphere, the splat quads write their depth and a mask (slot 31, r8) over their whole footprint, so the temporal pass reprojects them exactly as rocks at their distance, keeps their history unclipped (the neighbourhood clip is what destroyed a moving sub-pixel feature; with exact reprojection the history over splat pixels holds 0.95 of the current frame's energy, so the clip has nothing to fix), and shortens their history under motion (0.95 at rest to 0.3 at 4 px per frame; a splat is already a filtered footprint, so a short history keeps it sharp where a long one smeared it over five resampled frames). On the fixed-rate pan the dim specks now keep a median 0.72 / 0.68 of their peak and 0.86 / 0.85 of their 5x5 energy at 3 and 6 units of strafe per second (0.30 / 0.35 and 0.62 / 0.67 before any of this; 1.00 with the layer, which twinkled), 1.00 on a slow pan and at rest; static flicker is level with the FXAA-tuned build (3902 pixels changing by more than 48 levels in the dense belt view).
- SMAA 1x as the spatial anti-aliasing pass, default on top of the temporal pass, with FXAA kept as an option. The toggles split: F5 (--taa) switches the temporal pass, F9 (--spatial) cycles the spatial pass through off, FXAA and SMAA. SMAA is the reference algorithm (preset HIGH: threshold 0.1, 16 search steps, 8 diagonal steps, 25% corner rounding) ported to Slang as three fullscreen passes over the tone-mapped image, with the reference area and search textures shipped as byte arrays widened to RGBA8; the vertex-side offsets are computed per pixel and the texel-offset fetches shift the coordinate instead, since SPIR-V wants constant offsets. Luma is taken in gamma space, as the edge detection expects, from the sRGB intermediate. Against the tuned FXAA on the dense belt view with the temporal pass on: pixels changing by more than 48 levels between frames 3120 against 3900 (2990 with the original FXAA, which blurred the specks), far specks keep 0.93 of their no-AA luminance against 0.96, and the post pass costs 0.80 against 0.68 ms at 1080p (whole GPU frame 7.00 against 6.83 ms). The texture descriptor count of the conventional backend rises from 32 to 40 for the two lookup tables and the two SMAA intermediates.
- Pixel-sized post effects are tuned in a reference frame (the 1600x900 default window) and keep their pixel size in any other window: the chromatic fringe takes its radius from the reference aspect and converts its shift through the reference resolution, so a 3440x1440 borderless window no longer shows stars split into separate red, green and blue dots at the corners (the old uv-space cubic grew with both the resolution and the ultrawide corner radius, 15 pixels of fringe against 3 in the default window); the bloom tap spacing scales with the frame height instead, so the halo keeps its size relative to the picture. Angular effects (vignette, lens flare) already work in normalised coordinates; grain stays per pixel.
- Dear ImGui (1.91.9b, vendored under third_party/imgui) provides a control panel docked to the right edge, F12 to show it and --ui to start with it: frame statistics with a frame-time plot and the GPU pass breakdown, and every toggle the hotkeys reach, grouped as quality, anti-aliasing, belt, tone, camera and overlay; the capture key moves to F10. Layering: the ImGui core is platform-agnostic and lives with the application (src/app/ui.cpp builds the panel from AppState, now in app/app_state.hpp); the Win32 backend is compiled into the platform layer behind platform/overlay.hpp, which attaches it through a platform-internal message hook on the window (platform/win32/window_access.hpp) that also consumes clicks, wheel and typed keys while the panel wants them, so no OS type reaches the application; the draw lists are rendered by the renderer's own backend (shaders/ui.slang) into the swapchain pass after the present triangle, from a 4 MiB overlay region at the end of the dynamic heap, with ImDrawVert repacked into 32-byte records so the pointer layout is unambiguous and the font atlas in texture slot 36. Captures do not include the panel, since they read the frame before presentation.
- Belt dust as a scattering medium (F11, --dust): a view-ray march through the same analytic belt density the rock extinction uses (rings, gap, flared height), so the fine matter between the rocks glows where they are dense and thins at the edges; each sample takes sunlight through the belt, the planets' shadows and the rock transmittance map, and scatters it with a forward-peaked Henyey-Greenstein phase (g 0.55) mixed with an isotropic share (0.35) for multiple scattering and back-scatter; what lies behind is attenuated by the view transmittance (premultiplied blend after the atmospheres). The dust's own extinction is 0.08 of the rock coefficient: the rock extinction caps its sun path at one belt width, the view ray does not, and at the full coefficient an in-plane chord was opaque grey. Seen from outside the belt the column through the thin medium vanishes, so the density rises with the camera's distance from the slab (times 30 at six slab half-heights out), which turns the belt into a faint disc with the giant's shadow cut through it, the distant counterpart the user asked for, without a second code path. The march runs at half resolution (16 view samples, 2 sun samples, jittered per pixel and frame) into its own target and is composited with a depth-aware four-tap upsample, so the haze does not bleed across rock silhouettes; the temporal pass settles the jitter (frame-to-frame flicker is lower with the dust than without, since it fills the black between rocks). Cost at 1080p, 520k rocks: 0.8 ms in the belt, 0.3 ms in the far view (the full-resolution first cut was 3.9 ms). Brightness: the belt view gains 11% mean luminance, the far belt band 1.7 to 2x.

## Linux desktop port (2026-09-12)

Implemented in `5a757ae` on `linux-port`. The starting point built only the CPU layers on Linux;
CMake rejected the desktop demo, and NoGraphicsAPI rejected non-Windows presentation. The complete
demo now builds and renders on the Linux PC. Setup and repeatable commands are in [LINUX.md](LINUX.md).

### Platform and build

- Added `src/platform/linux/` implementations of all four platform interfaces. SDL2 handles Wayland
  or X11 windows, event polling, relative mouse look, key presses, focus, minimize, maximize and
  borderless fullscreen. The native handle passed through the renderer is an `SDL_Window*` on Linux.
- Vendored the SDL2 Dear ImGui backend from the same 1.91.9b tag as the existing core. SDL events feed
  ImGui before application input; mouse and keyboard capture prevent panel input from also driving
  the camera. Fontconfig selects regular/bold sans-serif fonts and FreeType rasterizes UTF-8 HUD text
  into the existing RGBA overlay format. Linux locates assets relative to `/proc/self/exe`.
- CMake selects the platform implementation and its dependencies. Added `linux-release`,
  `linux-debug` and `linux-cpu` presets, and `tools/bootstrap.sh`, which verifies the SHA-256 of the
  pinned Slang 2026.14.1 Linux archive before installing it under ignored `.tools/slang`.
  `spirv-val` validates generated shaders when installed. System Vulkan, SDL2, FreeType and Fontconfig
  development packages are documented separately from shader bootstrap.
- The NoGraphicsAPI package configuration also resolves SDL2 on Linux. Regenerated
  `third_party/NoGraphicsAPI-compat.patch` against upstream `8e414bd0a8010b9f721d06d470860e27aa69c071`
  and checked that applying it reproduces all four modified upstream files.

### Vulkan presentation and device compatibility

| Problem found on the Linux PC | Implemented change | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Presentation was guarded by `_WIN32`. | Obtain instance extensions from SDL, create the SDL Vulkan surface, and enable swapchain/maintenance extensions on Linux. | The existing renderer presents through native Wayland or XWayland. |
| Wayland reports `UINT_MAX` for the application-selected surface extent. | Query SDL's drawable size, clamp it to surface limits, and use that result for swapchain creation, resize detection and drawable-size queries. | Startup, resize and fullscreen work with compositor-selected pixel dimensions. A requested 960x540 window measured 961x540 on this desktop. |
| Mesa exposed 81 surface formats, exceeding the backend's fixed limit of 64. | Raise the format enumeration capacity to 256. | Device initialization reaches swapchain creation on this driver. |
| Device selection required 64-bit timestamps; this Intel queue reports 36 valid bits. | Accept queues with nonzero timestamp precision and expose `DeviceCaps::timestamp_valid_bits`. | The GPU is accepted and the renderer can account for counter wrap. |
| Intel lacks `shaderStorageImageReadWithoutFormat`, which Orbital's shaders do not use. | Make it optional for the conventional backend; enable it only when supported and expose `storage_image_read_without_format` in the device capabilities. | Device creation succeeds without claiming unsupported shader functionality. The experimental backend retains its requirement. |

### Renderer and shader corrections

- GPU pass timings now use `core/timing.hpp::timestamp_ticks` to mask timestamp differences to the
  device's valid counter width before converting ticks to milliseconds. This covers total GPU time
  and shadow, surface, atmosphere and post timings. Compile-time tests cover ordinary intervals,
  36-bit wrap, 64-bit wrap and a zero-width counter.
- Khronos validation reported calls to `vkCmdDrawIndexedIndirectCount` without the Vulkan 1.2
  `drawIndirectCount` feature enabled. Device selection now checks it, and device creation enables
  it. This is the existing compacted asteroid multi-draw path.
- Validation also reported Slang-generated `DemoteToHelperInvocation` capabilities without the
  matching Vulkan 1.3 feature. Device selection and creation now check and enable
  `shaderDemoteToHelperInvocation` for those fragment shaders.
- The shader texture arrays had 40 entries while the CPU's `Slot::count` was 38. Validation reported
  uninitialized descriptors starting at slot 38. `ORBITAL_TEXTURE_COUNT` in `scene_shared.h` now
  supplies the array size to `common.slang`, `cull.slang`, `ui.slang` and the CPU slot count. The
  existing fallback-binding loop consequently initializes reserved slots 38 and 39 as well.
- Explicitly initialized the pixel containers in two renderer image constructions to remove GCC
  missing-field warnings. The scene's lighting, atmospheric scattering, asteroid population,
  material model, tone mapping and anti-aliasing algorithms retain their existing behavior; the
  rendering changes above concern valid resource bindings, enabled features and timing accuracy.

- The belt has two representations blended by the camera's distance: full detail inside and near it (the rock meshes and splats, the half-resolution dust march), and a baked disc once the camera is out (fade from 0.5 to 2 belt widths past the slab, smoothstep, scaled by the panel's far-belt fade distance). The disc is two maps over the belt plane in an orthographic frame: the sunlight reaching the plane through the belt and the planets' shadows (1024 texels, baked every 16 frames while the far tier is in use), and the rocks' coverage times albedo, splatted from the rock records every 32 frames (256 texels so each texel averages many rocks; rocks above 0.06 world radius stay out of it and keep their splats and meshes, since they are rare and dominated their texel as bright blotches). The far tier is not a plane lookup, which breaks down edge-on, but a 12-sample march through the slab's thickness: the analytic density for the dust, the vertical profile applied to the baked rock coverage, the baked sunlight for both, sampled with at least a 3x3 box and wider with minification. The near paths carry the complementary weight (splat coverage and the dust march scale by one minus the weight), so the crossfade never pops. The distance-scaled density hack of the earlier dust pass is gone; in its place an explicit legibility boost ramps in with the weight (25x for the dust, 8x for the rock map, times the far-belt brightness slider), because at true scale the far belt is faint: the dust is thin and the rocks cover a fraction of a percent of the plane. Measured at 1080p, 520k rocks: far Jupiter view 5.55 ms against 5.32 with the old hack, the Earth view unchanged at 5.38, inside the belt unchanged.
- The far-belt disc and the near dust march now share one distance ramp (dustFarScale in belt.slang: density times up to 30 over six slab half-heights, times the far-belt brightness slider), and the crossfade sits past its saturation (one to two ramp lengths out, scaled by the fade-distance slider). The first cut removed the ramp from the near march when the disc LOD was on and gave the disc its own boosts (25x dust, 8x rocks) keyed to belt widths: just outside the belt the dust went nearly invisible, then brightened suddenly as the disc came in, brighter than the old look. With the shared ramp and no separate boosts the disc-on and disc-off images agree within 3 to 12 percent in the far views, so switching the LOD changes only the integration (noise-free, with the small-rock texture) and not the belt's brightness or shading; disc off reproduces the pre-LOD frames exactly.
- The far-belt disc is composited in the same pass as the near dust march, mixed by the LOD weight before one premultiplied blend. Stacked as two weighted layers, the belt's opacity mid-fade was the product of two partial opacities (an opaque belt dropped to 75 percent at the halfway point and recovered), which read as a translucency dip at a fixed distance. The sunlight bake now stores two transmittances, the dust's thin medium and the rocks' extinction, so each layer of the disc is shaded as its near counterpart is (the dust march and the splats), which removed a 26 percent brightness drop at the far end of the fade. Verified by sweeping the blend weight at a fixed far camera through --lod-scale: the belt band goes from 0.0053 to 0.0052 in even steps across the fade, and the Earth view with the disc matches the full-detail render (0.0256 against 0.0257).
- Presentation mode is switchable (DeviceDesc::vsync and set_vsync in the fork, --vsync and a panel switch in the app; benchmarks run with it off unless told otherwise). Upstream hard-codes FIFO. On a 60 Hz display that holds every frame to 16.7 ms, and a GPU that finishes early idles and drops its clocks, so its timestamps come out no faster than a slower card's: an RTX 4080 on a 60 Hz panel reported the same GPU times as the GTX 1080 Ti on its 165 Hz panel. Off selects mailbox where the surface offers it, else immediate. Verified on the 1080 Ti: the moon view goes from 166 fps (the display) to 526 fps uncapped, with the GPU time unchanged at 1.4 ms, since this display never let the GPU idle.
- With vsync off the fork now prefers immediate presentation over mailbox: in a window the desktop compositor can still throttle a mailbox swapchain to the display, which is what a 60 Hz cap that survives the switch looks like. The renderer logs the presentation mode and image count the driver granted whenever they change (get_swapchain_info, another conventional-only addition), so a capped machine can be diagnosed from its console: FIFO after --vsync 0 means the surface offered neither unsynchronized mode; immediate and still capped points outside the application (a driver-level vsync or frame limiter, or a hybrid-graphics path through an integrated GPU).
- Earth sea state and cloud shadows are panel tweakables (an Earth section) carried in two frame float4s. The ocean's GGX roughness rises from 0.085 to 0.18, the Cox-Munk slope variance of a moderate wind, so the sun's reflection is a sun-glitter patch rather than a hard white dot, and a slow wind field modulates it (sea patchiness) so the glint breaks into brighter and calmer stretches; glint intensity scales the sea's specular only. Cloud shadows read the cloud mask at a coarser mip (softness, 1.5 mips by default) without the magnification detail, so they are softer than the clouds that cast them, as the sun's disc and in-cloud scattering make them; their strength (0.6) and the cloud layer's opacity (0.88) are exposed too. Defaults reproduce the previous look except for the wider glint and the softer shadows.
